# Risks, stated plainly

*Companion to [`memo.md`](./memo.md). An investor will find these anyway;
finding them here first is the point.*

## Distribution is the whole game
The product is complete; nobody knows it exists. There is no marketing
history, no channel with proven CAC, no waitlist. The bet is that the
species-science content engine plus the free adoption network can acquire
owners cheaply — that bet is untested. **Mitigation:** the blog engine and
half the content already exist in the repo; the first funded workstream is
publishing and measuring, not building.

## Two-sided surfaces start empty
The marketplace has no sellers and the pro directory has no professionals
yet. Empty supply pages are designed honestly (no fake listings), but an
empty marketplace earns nothing. **Mitigation:** first-party curated products
can seed the shop without third-party sellers; pro onboarding is a manual,
founder-led sales motion in one city before it is a funnel.

## Retention depends on a daily habit
The twin is only as good as the check-in streak. Habit products live or die
on week-4 retention, and there is no retention data yet. **Mitigation:** the
check-in is deliberately under a minute, reminders exist, and the record's
value compounds — but this is the number to watch first, and it may be bad.

## The memorial category is ethically loaded
Likeness and persona features (twin stages 3–4) monetize grief. Done
carelessly this destroys the brand. **Mitigation:** the design doc commits to
explicit consent, hard guardrails, and a written "would not build" list —
and the category ships only after the living-pet product has trust.

## Solo-founder concentration
One person, many products. The studio system (shared packages, CI, one
design system) is what makes that tractable, but bus factor is 1 and investor
diligence will price that. **Mitigation:** unusually high automation, every
product CI-gated and self-deploying; capital can buy the first hire.

## Platform scope invites the "focus" question
Health + booking + commerce + adoption in one product reads as four startups.
The honest answer: the health record is the product; the other three are
distribution and monetization attached to it, all built on shared
infrastructure that cost days, not quarters. If a surface fails to earn its
place, it is cheap to shut off.

## Market figures are third-party estimates
The memo quotes ranges (pet care $180–270B, pet tech $9–18B, 2025) because
analyst reports disagree by scope. No figure in the pitch is our own
extrapolation.
